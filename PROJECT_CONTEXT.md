## Working Title

Pokemon Mystery Dungeon: Explorers of Legends

## Core Identity

This is a Pokemon Mystery Dungeon-inspired project centered on the question:

What does it mean to become a legend?

The story should avoid the default PMD structure of "human turns into a Pokemon, prophecy, world-ending disaster, legendary final boss" unless those tropes are intentionally remixed. The protagonist and partner are Pokemon from the start. They earn importance through deeds, renown, choices, skill growth, and domain trials.

The project should feel classic PMD: emotional, heartfelt, adventurous, and character-driven. It can include political and mythic conflict, but the emotional tone should stay warm and earnest rather than grim.

## Confirmed User Decisions

- The player character is a Pokemon from the start, not a transformed human.
- The partner is overly ambitious, headstrong, emotional, and wants to be remembered.
- The story is about earning the title of legend.
- Original legendary and mythical Pokemon were ancient gods of separate domains.
- These original gods are not playable in the main story. They appear in flashbacks, records, myths, or postgame encounters.
- Their roles are now inherited as domain titles or Mantles by worthy successor Pokemon.
- Current successors are called Living Legends.
- The protagonist and partner travel through each legendary domain and clear a main quest in each.
- Darkrai, Giratina, and Yveltal, or their descendants/successors, should be major overarching villain forces.
- A rival team should cut corners and do whatever it takes to earn legendary status.
- The game should keep standard Pokemon leveling.
- Leveling and major progress should grant attribute points that can be invested into Pokemon stats such as HP, Attack, Defense, Special Attack, Special Defense, and Speed.
- RuneScape-style non-combat skills are wanted at medium depth.
- Skills should be hidden behind ranks rather than exposed as visible numbers.
- Questlines connected to Renown are important.
- Optional/random questlines should appear from time to time.
- Branching endings should depend on Renown and choices.
- Starter selection should include a personality quiz.
- Include every Pokemon generation if possible, including Mega Evolutions.
- No fakemon.
- Regional forms are allowed.
- Use only official Pokemon.
- Legendaries should only become available postgame.
- Charisma should affect social renown and social outcomes.
- Story scenes should focus on only the protagonist and partner as the main characters.

## Design Pillars

1. Legends are inherited responsibilities, not just species categories.
2. Ordinary Pokemon can earn legendary status through deeds.
3. The player builds a life and reputation, not just a rescue rank.
4. Dungeons support multiple approaches: combat, gathering, social choices, lore, puzzles, survival, and crafting.
5. Renown and hidden skill ranks shape quests, endings, NPC reactions, and domain trials.
6. The story should be emotional first, with mythic scale supporting character arcs.

## Core Lore: Domains and Mantles

The First Legends were the original legendary and mythical Pokemon. Each represented a sacred domain and left behind a Mantle: a spiritual title, duty, and source of authority tied to that domain.

A Pokemon who inherits a Mantle is called a Living Legend. Living Legends are not gods, but they carry a piece of divine responsibility and are expected to protect, judge, or guide their domain.

The protagonist and partner do not start with a Mantle. They begin as ordinary adventurers and become candidates through repeated deeds.

## Main Story Direction

The world is divided into sacred domains once ruled by the First Legends. Those ancient beings are gone, sealed, sleeping, dead, ascended, or visible only through memory and flashback. Their Mantles now pass to worthy Pokemon.

The Mantle system is breaking:

- Some Living Legends are arrogant.
- Some are exhausted.
- Some were chosen unfairly.
- Some inherited power through politics instead of heroism.
- Some ambitious Pokemon believe legendhood should belong to whoever is strong enough to seize it.

The protagonist and their headstrong partner travel from domain to domain, solving crises, meeting current Living Legends, earning Renown, and deciding what kind of legend is worth becoming.

## Partner Arc

The partner is ambitious, emotional, stubborn, and headstrong. They do not just want to help people; they want to be remembered.

Early attitude:

"One day, they're going to carve our names beside the legends."

Their flaw is insecurity, not evil. They believe being unknown means being worthless.

Recommended arc:

- Early game: "I want to become a legend so everyone will know my name."
- Mid game: "Some legends are fake. Some are cruel. What if becoming one changes us?"
- Late game: "A real legend protects others even when no one remembers."
- Ending: The partner earns a Mantle, rejects the title, helps redefine legendhood, or learns humility depending on choices.

## Rival Team

Recommended name: Team Apex

Other possible names:

- Team Ascendant
- Team Crownfall
- Team Dominion
- Team Renegade

Team Apex is the dark mirror of the protagonist and partner. They want legendary status at any cost.

They may:

- Steal credit for missions.
- Abandon weaker Pokemon in dungeons.
- Take shortcuts through forbidden domain shrines.
- Manipulate towns into thinking they solved crises.
- Accept power from corrupted Mantles.
- Sabotage the protagonist's reputation.

Their leader should be charismatic, skilled, and genuinely impressive, not a cartoon villain. Their philosophy:

"Legends aren't chosen because they're kind. They're chosen because they win."

Team Apex should become tragic when they try to force a Mantle inheritance before they are worthy.

## Overarching Villains

Darkrai, Giratina, and Yveltal should be represented through original gods, inherited Mantles, descendants, or corrupted successors.

Recommended antagonist group: The Triad of Ending

| First Legend | Domain | Possible Current Villain Successor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Darkrai | Nightmare | Malamar, Zoroark, Spiritomb, Weavile |
| Giratina | Distortion / Void | Dusknoir, Spiritomb, Banette, Sableye |
| Yveltal | Death / Ending | Absol, Honchkrow, Houndoom, Mandibuzz |

The Triad of Ending believes the current Mantle system is weak. They want to create a world where legendhood is seized through dominance instead of earned through service.

Their final plan can be to collapse all domains into one final Mantle: the Legend of Dominion.

Important nuance: the "dark" First Legends were not originally evil. Death, nightmares, and distortion were necessary domains. Their successors became corrupted because the world feared those domains instead of understanding them.

## Campaign Structure

Each domain arc should include:

- A new region or town.
- A domain crisis.
- A current Living Legend successor.
- A main Mystery Dungeon.
- Optional Renown quests.
- A domain trial.
- A title, relic, or recognition gained by the player team.

Suggested act flow:

### Act 1: The First Name

The protagonist and partner form a team in a small town, complete early rescue jobs, and join a guild. The partner wants fame immediately, while the protagonist grounds them.

The first major dungeon introduces Renown. A mentor explains that ordinary Pokemon can become legends, but only through deeds recognized by the world.

### Act 2: The Domain Pilgrimage

The team travels across domains, solving crises and learning different lessons about legendhood.

Possible domain order:

1. Seed: growth, patience, community.
2. Storm: courage under chaos.
3. Dream: empathy and inner fear.
4. Land: duty and endurance.
5. Sky: freedom and responsibility.
6. Life: mercy.
7. Death: grief and acceptance.
8. Space: perspective and humility.
9. Time: consequence and sacrifice.

Time should be late-game because Dialga's legacy is too important to spend early.

### Act 3: Team Apex Rises

Team Apex earns recognition faster than the player by taking shortcuts. The partner becomes jealous and frustrated:

"We're helping everyone the right way. So why are they the ones being called legends?"

Optional quests and Renown should matter here. If the player has built trust, NPCs begin quietly defending the team.

### Act 4: The Corrupted Mantles

The Triad of Ending reveals that Mantles can be stolen, broken, or fused. Team Apex tries to force a Mantle inheritance before they are worthy. The ritual goes wrong and binds them to a corrupted domain.

### Act 5: The Trial of Legends

The protagonist and partner are summoned to the ancient place where the First Legends once gathered. They see flashbacks of the original gods:

- Dialga shaping time.
- Palkia dividing space.
- Xerneas breathing life into barren lands.
- Yveltal accepting death as necessary balance.
- Darkrai guarding nightmares before becoming feared.
- Giratina holding the world's distortions and being exiled.

### Final Act: The Mantle War

The Triad attempts to create the Legend of Dominion. Team Apex may join them, be betrayed by them, or help stop them depending on player choices.

Final dungeon concept: The Hall of First Legends

This dungeon is made of broken pieces of every domain. Floors shift between Seed, Storm, Dream, Land, Sky, Life, Death, Space, and Time. Previous choices determine which domain spirits help or hinder the player.

## Branching Endings

Endings should depend on Renown, domain choices, partner bond, and how the player treats Team Apex.

Possible endings:

- The New Accord: Reform the Mantle system so towns, guilds, and domain guardians all have a voice. Best true ending candidate.
- The People's Legend: Reject formal Mantles and prove ordinary Pokemon can protect the world without divine titles.
- The Crowned Legend: The protagonist or partner accepts a Mantle and becomes a Living Legend.
- The Forgotten Hero: Save the world but refuse recognition. Strong humility ending.
- The Apex Redemption: Team Apex gives up stolen power and helps in the final battle. Requires earlier mercy choices.
- The Hollow Legend: Bad or bittersweet ending where the team gains status but loses trust, bond, or moral standing.

## RPG Progression

### Standard Leveling

Keep normal Pokemon leveling:

- Experience.
- Level-ups.
- Move learning.
- Evolution where applicable.
- Natural stat growth.

### Attribute Points

Attribute Points should be earned from:

- Level-ups.
- Major quest completion.
- Renown milestones.
- Domain trials.

Spend Attribute Points on:

- HP
- Attack
- Defense
- Special Attack
- Special Defense
- Speed

Use soft caps based on species, evolution stage, and role so investment cannot completely erase Pokemon identity.

### Hidden Skill Ranks

Use hidden ranks instead of visible levels.

Rank ladder:

- Novice
- Apprentice
- Adept
- Expert
- Master
- Grandmaster, mostly postgame

Player-facing messaging should be qualitative:

- "Your team's Foraging skill has improved."
- "You feel more confident reading ancient inscriptions."

## Renown

Renown should be split into types rather than one generic fame meter.

Possible types:

- Heroic Renown: rescues, protection, honorable choices.
- Scholar Renown: lore, ruins, discoveries.
- Outlaw Renown: intimidation, shady quests, selfish choices.
- Diplomat Renown: peaceful resolutions.
- Explorer Renown: dungeon completion, mapmaking, survival.
- Legend Renown: major story deeds, domain trials, legendary recognition.
- Social Renown: charisma-driven town trust, bonds, and public favor.

Renown can unlock:

- Guild ranks.
- Domain trials.
- Legendary mentor quests.
- Restricted dungeons.
- Rare crafting recipes.
- Alternate quest solutions.
- Branching ending points.
- Team titles.
- Town upgrades.
- NPC support in story scenes.

## Optional and Random Questlines

Optional questlines should appear based on:

- Current domain.
- Team Renown.
- Skill ranks.
- Partner bond.
- Previous choices.
- Rival progress.
- Current chapter.

These quests should never hard-block the main story. They should add shortcuts, rewards, hidden lore, improved endings, or alternate conflict resolutions.

Example Seed Domain quest:

A village needs rare medicinal herbs.

Possible solutions:

- Foraging finds herbs.
- Cooking turns them into medicine.
- Diplomacy convinces another town to share supplies.
- Combat clears the dungeon where herbs grow.

Example Storm Domain quest:

A lightning shrine is overcharging.

Possible solutions:

- Tactics and Trapcraft redirect energy.
- Lore explains the shrine's old purpose.
- Combat defeats the Pokemon abusing the shrine.
- Charisma convinces the shrine guardian to cooperate.

Example Dream Domain quest:

A child Pokemon is trapped in recurring nightmares.

Possible solutions:

- Dream dungeon combat.
- Insight identifies the real fear.
- Performance calms the dream.
- Lore explains Cresselia's old blessing.

## Engine and Build Direction

### Best fit for the full design: PMDO-style fangame

Best if the goal is custom systems: attribute points, hidden skill ranks, Renown, branching endings, charisma, random optional questlines, all generations, regional forms, Megas, and every domain arc.

Pros:

- Strongest fit for custom RPG systems.
- Easier to add Renown, hidden ranks, custom menus, branching questlines, and attribute point logic.
- PC release is easier than DS ROM hacking.
- Better flexibility for all generations and Megas.

Cons:

- Less authentic to original DS PMD feel.
- Requires more custom content work.
- Still has Pokemon IP risk if using Pokemon names, designs, or assets.
- Needs programming/scripting comfort.

Realism: high for the desired full design.

### SkyTemple / Explorers of Sky ROM hack

Best if the goal is authentic PMD feel and a story-focused hack.

Pros:

- Most authentic classic PMD feel.
- Existing PMD dungeon and battle engine.
- Strong fit for emotional story scenes.

Cons:

- Deep systems may be difficult.
- Attribute points, hidden ranks, random optional quests, and branching endings may be technically hard.
- Every generation plus Megas is a major stretch.
- DS engine limits apply.
- Distribution must avoid ROM files and still carries IP risk.

Realism: medium.

### pmd-sky decompilation route

Best for deep control, but very technical.

Pros:

- More control than editor-only ROM hacking.
- Better chance of implementing custom systems directly.

Cons:

- Requires reverse engineering, C, assembly, build tools, and DS internals knowledge.
- Not ideal as a first route for a small team.
- Same IP risks.

Realism: low to medium.

### RPG Maker XP + Pokemon Essentials

Better for a traditional Pokemon fangame than a true PMD game.

Pros:

- Good for story, towns, quests, dialogue, and branching.
- Large fan community.

Cons:

- Built for mainline Pokemon structure, not Mystery Dungeon.
- PMD dungeon/combat would need heavy custom work or approximation.
- May feel like a normal Pokemon RPG instead of PMD.
- Same IP risks.

Realism: medium for normal Pokemon RPG, low to medium for true PMD.

### Godot / Unity / custom original engine

Best long-term route if rebranded as original IP.

Pros:

- Maximum freedom.
- Best legal safety if Pokemon is replaced with original creatures, setting, and terminology.
- No ROM hacking limitations.

Cons:

- Most work.
- Requires building battle systems, dungeon generation, AI, UI, save systems, moves, statuses, and tools.
- IP risk remains if Pokemon names/designs/assets are used.

Realism: medium for original IP, low for recreating a full Pokemon roster and PMD system from scratch.

## Legal and Copyright Notes

This is not legal advice.

Public Pokemon fan games and ROM hacks carry real copyright and trademark risk, even if free.

Do not:

- Distribute ROM files.
- Share full DS/GBA ROMs, modified ROMs, pre-patched ROMs, BIOS files, or dumped game assets.
- Use official Pokemon branding as if authorized.
- Use official logos, box art, or wording implying endorsement by Nintendo, Game Freak, The Pokemon Company, or Spike Chunsoft.
- Monetize access through sales, ads, paid downloads, Patreon rewards, merch, paid beta keys, or paid Discord access.
- Use official music, sound effects, sprites, portraits, tiles, UI, dialogue, or ripped assets.
- Rely on "fair use" or disclaimers as protection.
- Use unreleased, leaked, or datamined official content.

Lower-risk community practice for ROM hacks:

- Release patch files only, not ROMs.
- Require players to provide their own legally obtained copy.
- Use original music and artwork where possible.
- Avoid official logos.
- Avoid money completely.
- Keep the project clearly unofficial.
- Keep scope and visibility modest.

Safest public-release path:

- Rework into original IP inspired by PMD.
- Use original creatures, title, setting, music, art, and terminology.
- Preserve the emotional dungeon-rescue structure and domain/legend succession themes without Pokemon-specific names or designs.

Possible original-IP title directions:

- Mystery Domains: Heirs of Legend
- Explorers of the First Legends

Current recommendation from the migrated chat:

- Creatively, continue designing the Pokemon version for private planning/prototyping.
- Technically, use a PMDO-style approach if the full RPG system is the priority.
- Legally, decide early whether this is private/personal, patch-only with risk, or a safer public original-IP project.
