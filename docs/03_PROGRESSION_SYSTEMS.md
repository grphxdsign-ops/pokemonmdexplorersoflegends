# Progression Systems

## Attribute Points

Attribute points can be spent on:

- HP
- Attack
- Defense
- Special Attack
- Special Defense
- Speed

Point costs are equal across the board for every Pokemon.

Do not use species-based scaling where defensive Pokemon pay more for Speed or similar. That idea is rejected.

## Speed Interpretation

Speed should follow the later PMD-style interpretation where Speed affects accuracy and evasion-style outcomes, not mainline Pokemon turn order.

Research note:

Bulbapedia's Mystery Dungeon stat page states that the Speed stat was introduced in Super Mystery Dungeon as an accuracy modifier: higher Speed makes a Pokemon more likely to hit and more likely to evade attacks. It also separates this from Movement Speed / Travel Speed, which governs action or tile movement behavior.

Source:

https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Stat_%28Mystery_Dungeon%29

Design lock:

- Speed affects hit reliability and evasion.
- Speed does not become a normal mainline turn-order stat.
- Movement Speed / Travel Speed remains a separate temporary status-style concept.
- Attribute point investment in Speed has the same cost as any other stat.

## Attribute Point Sources

Attribute points should be awarded from:

- level-ups,
- domain quest completions,
- major story milestones,
- Renown rank increases,
- special training,
- rare postgame rewards.

Early tuning target:

- small gains from level-ups,
- meaningful gains from domain milestones,
- limited enough that choices matter,
- generous enough that the player feels RPG growth.

## Hidden Skill Ranks

Skills use hidden ranks, not visible levels.

Rank ladder:

- Novice
- Apprentice
- Adept
- Expert
- Master
- Grandmaster

The player should receive qualitative feedback:

- "Your team has become more confident at Foraging."
- "Your Survival instincts sharpen."
- "You can now read older inscription marks."
- "Your team has reached Adept rank in First Aid."

## Renown Types

Renown should track how the team is becoming known.

Types:

- Heroic Renown
- Scholar Renown
- Diplomat Renown
- Explorer Renown
- Social Renown
- Outlaw Renown
- Legend Renown
- Domain-specific Renown

Social Renown is affected by Charisma.

Renown should influence:

- dialogue,
- optional quests,
- domain trust,
- Team Apex comparisons,
- partner moral drift,
- climax allies,
- ending variants,
- postgame access.

## Partner Moral Drift System

The partner should have an internal moral stability track.

This does not need to be shown as a meter.

Increases when:

- the player chooses mercy,
- the player avoids taking false credit,
- the player helps ordinary Pokemon,
- the player calls out Team Apex,
- the player resolves conflicts without humiliation,
- the player builds Social or Diplomat Renown.

Decreases when:

- the player chooses shortcuts,
- the player accepts selfish rewards,
- the player intimidates vulnerable NPCs,
- the player takes credit unfairly,
- the player repeatedly prioritizes fame,
- Team Apex publicly outpaces the team.

At the Tower of Legends, the partner should face a temptation scene. The protagonist can pull them back, but the tone and dialogue should change based on prior moral stability.

## Branching Ending Inputs

Endings should consider:

- total Legend Renown,
- partner moral stability,
- treatment of Team Apex,
- whether Dusknoir was trusted or condemned,
- number of domain alliances built,
- Social Renown,
- Outlaw Renown,
- optional quest completion,
- whether the player prioritized titles or service.

The current main ending is fixed around the protagonist's sacrifice and resurrection. Branching should alter:

- who arrives at the summit,
- how the partner reacts,
- what happens to Team Apex,
- how domains interpret the final event,
- postgame political state,
- whether the team accepts, rejects, or redefines legendhood.
